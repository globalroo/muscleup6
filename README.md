# Muscle Up trial

Cycles for workout progressions

Below is a 12-week, 3-day-per-week gym program designed to build muscle mass (“muscle up”) and incorporate some cardio to help reduce body fat. The routine uses standard gym equipment and is split into three phases (each four weeks long). Technique guidelines are provided for each main exercise, and you can add short cardio sessions on one or two non-lifting days or after your workout if time permits.

---

## Overview

- **Frequency**: 3 x weight-training sessions/week  
- **Split**: Push / Pull / Legs  
- **Phases**:  
  - **Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4)**: Foundation & Hypertrophy  
  - **Phase 2 (Weeks 5–8)**: Strength-Focused Hypertrophy  
  - **Phase 3 (Weeks 9–12)**: Higher Volume & Intensity

- **Rest**: 60–90 seconds between sets (Phase 1), 90–120 seconds (Phase 2), 60–90 seconds (Phase 3)  
- **Progression**: Aim to add a little weight (2.5–5 lbs / 1–2 kg) or 1–2 reps each week when you can do all sets at the prescribed rep range comfortably.  
- **Cardio**: 20–30 minutes of moderate-intensity or HIIT 1–2x/week. This can be on rest days or after your workout.  

---

# Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4)

**Goals:**  
- Establish proper exercise form  
- Begin progressive overload for hypertrophy  
- Moderate volume (3 sets), moderate rep ranges (8–12)

### Weekly Schedule

- **Monday: Push (Chest, Shoulders, Triceps)**  
- **Wednesday: Pull (Back, Biceps)**  
- **Friday: Legs (Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes)**  

> *If needed, adjust days to fit your schedule, but ensure at least 1 day of rest between sessions.*

---

## Day 1: Push

1. **Barbell Bench Press**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Keep feet flat on the floor, arch your lower back slightly.  
     - Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width.  
     - Lower the bar to mid-chest, then press up until arms are nearly locked out.

2. **Dumbbell Shoulder Press**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Sit upright on a bench with back support.  
     - Start with dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing forward.  
     - Press overhead, keeping a slight bend in elbows at the top.

3. **Incline Dumbbell Flyes**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Lie on an incline bench (30–45°).  
     - Maintain a slight bend in elbows; open arms wide and then bring dumbbells together above your chest in an arc.

4. **Cable Triceps Pushdowns**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 10–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Use a cable machine with a straight or rope attachment.  
     - Keep elbows pinned at your sides and extend your arms down.  
     - Avoid flaring your elbows outward.

5. **Optional: Lateral Raises**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 2–3 × 10–12 (if time and energy allow)  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Stand upright with a dumbbell in each hand.  
     - Lift arms out to your sides, stopping at shoulder height.  
     - Keep a slight bend in the elbows.

---

## Day 2: Pull

1. **Deadlift**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–10  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Stand with feet hip-width apart, bar over mid-foot.  
     - Hinge at the hips, grip the bar just outside your knees.  
     - Keep your back neutral, drive through your heels to stand upright.

2. **Lat Pulldown**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width.  
     - Lean back slightly, pull elbows down and back.  
     - Aim bar toward upper chest, then return to start under control.

3. **Seated Cable Row**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Keep your back straight, core tight.  
     - Pull the handle to your lower rib cage while squeezing shoulder blades together.  
     - Control the return phase.

4. **Dumbbell Biceps Curls**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Keep elbows tucked at your sides.  
     - Curl dumbbells up toward your shoulders without swinging.  
     - Lower them slowly.

5. **Face Pulls**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 2–3 × 10–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Use a rope attachment on a cable at chest or eye-level.  
     - Pull the rope toward your forehead, flaring elbows out.  
     - Focus on squeezing the upper back and rear delts.

---

## Day 3: Legs

1. **Back Squat**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Feet shoulder-width apart, bar resting on your upper back (traps).  
     - Keep chest up, core tight; sit back and down as if on a chair.  
     - Drive through heels to stand up.

2. **Leg Press**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Adjust seat so knees can bend to about 90° at the bottom.  
     - Push through the whole foot, especially heels.  
     - Avoid locking out the knees completely.

3. **Walking Lunges**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 10–12 per leg  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Keep torso upright, core braced.  
     - Step forward, lower your back knee close to the ground.  
     - Push back up through the front heel.

4. **Romanian Deadlift (RDL)**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 3 × 8–12  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Hold a barbell or dumbbells in front of thighs.  
     - Keep knees slightly bent, hinge at hips lowering the weight down your shins.  
     - Squeeze glutes to return upright.

5. **Calf Raises**  
   - **Sets x Reps**: 2–3 × 12–15  
   - **Technique**:  
     - Use a standing or seated calf machine.  
     - Pause briefly at the top for a peak contraction.

# Phase 2 (Weeks 5–8)

**Goals:**  
- Increase the load (or reduce reps slightly to focus on strength)  
- Continue progressive overload  
- Slightly longer rest periods (90–120 seconds)  
- Volume adjustments: 4 sets for main movements, 3 sets for accessories  

### Changes from Phase 1

- **Main Compound Lifts** (Bench Press, Deadlift, Squat): Move to **4 sets** of **6–8 reps**.  
- **Other Exercises**: Move to **3 sets** of **8–10** reps.  
- Maintain good form and continue to increase weight gradually where possible.

**Cardio**: You can add 1–2 sessions/week of 20–30 minutes moderate-intensity cardio or add a short HIIT session (e.g., 5–8 intervals of 20s sprint/40s rest).

---

# Phase 3 (Weeks 9–12)

**Goals:**  
- Increase training intensity and volume for final hypertrophy push  
- Emphasize mind-muscle connection and controlled eccentric (lowering) phases  
- Reduce rest periods slightly back to 60–90 seconds to keep intensity high  

### Changes from Phase 2

- **Main Compound Lifts**: 4 sets of **8–10 reps**, focusing on a controlled negative (2–3 seconds down).  
- **Other Exercises**: 3–4 sets of **10–12 reps** with short rest (60–90 seconds).  
- **Optional Intensifiers** (use sparingly): drop sets, rest-pause, or supersets on the last set of accessory exercises.

**Cardio**: Same as previous phases, or slightly increase intensity/duration if fat loss is a priority (but ensure adequate recovery).

# Technique & Form Resources

While we can’t embed direct video links here, you can find excellent exercise demonstrations on platforms like YouTube or official fitness websites. Search for the exercise name plus “proper form” (e.g., “Barbell Bench Press proper form”) to view reputable trainers and channels. Always check multiple sources if you’re unsure.

**Key Points for Safe Lifting**:  
1. **Warm up**: 5–10 minutes of light cardio + 1–2 warmup sets with lighter weights before heavy lifts.  
2. **Core engagement**: Keep your core braced and spine neutral in all exercises.  
3. **Full range of motion**: Use a controlled tempo, especially in the eccentric (lowering) phase.  
4. **Progressive Overload**: Gradually increase weights or reps over time.  
5. **Listen to your body**: If something causes joint pain, reduce load or modify range of motion.

---

# Additional Tips for Success

1. **Nutrition**: To build muscle mass, aim for a moderate calorie surplus (slightly above maintenance) with adequate protein (1.2–1.6 g protein per kg bodyweight, or about 0.6–0.8 g per lb).  
2. **Recovery**: Prioritize sleep (7–9 hours/night) and incorporate active rest or light mobility work on non-training days.  
3. **Tracking**: Keep a training log (sets, reps, weights used). Progress tracking helps maintain motivation and ensure overload.  
4. **Consistency**: The biggest results come from consistency. Stick to the program, progress weights sensibly, and stay patient.

## Summary

This 12-week, 3-day-per-week “Muscle Up” program combines compound and accessory exercises with progressive overload to help you build muscle mass while incorporating moderate cardio to assist with fat loss. Focus on mastering technique, tracking your progress, and consistently challenging yourself with slightly heavier weights or extra reps over time. By the end of 12 weeks, you should see improvements in strength, muscle size, and body composition.

**Good luck, and lift safely!**
